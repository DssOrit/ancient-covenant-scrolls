## M. Required expansion (owner correction)
A first-class feature, NOT an optional supplement to government contacts. Help users prepare independent plans and find potential lawful off-grid options even where public assistance cannot be reached. Government maps are evidence sources and alert authorities, not the only resource category. Must be useful during extended grid failure, loss of running water, road interruption, or isolation on private property.

### M4. Legal distinction, US vs Portugal
- United States: BLM allows dispersed camping on many but not all BLM lands; limits and closures differ by field office. USFS and state park/forest permissions are land-specific. No general right to camp on every woodland, riverside, park or beach. BLM overview: https://www.blm.gov/programs/recreation/camping ; local forest rules: https://www.fs.usda.gov/
- Portugal: do not import US dispersed-camping assumptions. Highway Code Article 50-A restricts overnight motorhome stays in Natura 2000, protected and designated coastal zones except where authorised, and regulates stays elsewhere under municipal rules. Motorhome overnight rules are NOT permission to pitch tents or wild camp. Legal text: https://www.segurancarodoviaria.pt/en/highway-code/title-ii-transit-of-vehicles-and-animals/chapter-i-common-provisions/section-v-some-maneuvers-in-particular/subsection-vi-stopping-and-parking/article-50-a-prohibition-of-overnight-stays-and-parking-of-motorhomes/ ; tourism guidance: https://www.visitportugal.com/en/content/campervanning . Get current municipal and land-manager rules before publishing a place as legal for an activity.
- Never suggest sheltering in riverbeds, caves subject to flash flooding, restricted forests during wildfire alerts, harbors with tsunami risk, or closed parks.

### M5. Off-grid advice cards (online AND offline, printable as two smaller PDFs)
1. 72 hours / 2 weeks / 3 months / 1 year independence planner. Counts for adults, seniors, pregnancy, children, disabilities, pets, and water for sanitation. Show assumptions; not medical prescriptions.
2. No electricity: insulated safe room, layers, sleeping pads, cool-room setup for heat, solar/battery sizing worksheet, critical circuits, fridge/freezer food handling. Generators stay outdoors; CO detectors needed.
3. No running water: store potable water before the event; collect rainwater only where lawful, stored separately as nonpotable; first-flush and roof contamination caveats; sealed containers and professionally tested wells. Microbe control is not chemical removal. CDC: https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/about/ ; EPA: https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/emergency-disinfection-drinking-water
4. Toilets and waste: portable toilet and containment, hand hygiene, dump only at authorised facilities; no dumping or burial near waterways.
5. Food: shelf-stable inventory, can opener, safe storage temperatures, container gardening and seed starting, approved outdoor cooking appliances. Foraging/fishing only with permissions, species ID, advisories, seasons and permits verified.
6. Communications: offline maps and contacts, printed routes, battery radio, power banks, spare batteries, check-in plan, accessible signalling. Satellite only if device and subscription are verified.
7. Road and terrain: low grades, avoid narrow/high bridges; unverified-grades notice unless backed by elevation and road data; no route promise in wildfire, flood, hurricane or tsunami.
8. Accessible independence: for elderly and pregnant users prioritise accessible toilets, sanitary drinking water, vehicle approach, temperature, clinical needs, medicines, mobility equipment and short distance to medical services; never prefer remote wilderness over a safer staffed destination. Oxygen, powered mobility, insulin refrigeration: individual backup-power plan and early-relocation triggers. Dogs: pet-friendly confirmation, safe temperature, leash/carrier, food and water.
